Page 6 · The first 60 days

Concrete. Dated. Sequenced.

Specific Functions named, specific accounts hit, specific decisions made by specific dates. Compressed to 60 because the system can ship faster than a quarter – the discipline is the same Andrew described publicly for Solidroad's engineering streams, applied to GTM.

The frame

Two months, three outcomes.

By day 60, three things are true – or the role isn't working and we both know it.

1 · The system is live

All six Clay Functions live and running. The three-table ABM architecture humming. Claude MCP wired into the operator's daily workflow. HubSpot is the source of truth. RevOps maintains the playbook; the operator runs it.

2 · Pipeline is real

80+ Aware-stage accounts active. 15+ Interested-stage. 3+ Evaluating-stage. At least one POC in motion that the operator surfaced and the closing team converted.

3 · The cadence holds

Weekly review with the Head of GTM happening, on schedule, with numbers and named accounts. Experiments killed on the dates they were scheduled to be killed. Documentation kept current as a deliverable, not an afterthought.

Days 1–30 · ship and run

Month one. Foundation in week one. First cohort by week four.

Week 1 · Onboard, access, ship all six Functions

  • Day 1. Standup with the Head of GTM. Lay of the land: current pipeline, existing customers, the motion already running. Meet the engineering, design, and customer teams. Quick coffee with whoever owns customer success – their conversations are signal-rich.
  • Day 2. Tool access: Clay workspace, HubSpot full access, Claude desktop with MCP wired in, Slack channels, Gong / Fireflies, sequencer, LinkedIn Sales Navigator. Get the access friction out of the way in day one, not day ten.
  • Day 2–3. Pipeline audit. Read every closed-won deal of the last 6 months. Read every closed-lost from the last 90 days. Listen to 10 recorded calls. Identify the 3 signal patterns that show up most in closed-won.
  • Days 3–5. Ship all six Clay Functions, in order: Account Enrichment, CX Persona Mapper, Signal Scoring, Outreach Drafter, Job-Change Detector, LinkedIn Engagement Harvester. Each one built, tested across 20 sample accounts, signed off by RevOps before moving to the next.
  • End of week 1. All six Functions live. One-pager submitted to the Head of GTM: "Here are the signal patterns I'm seeing in your closed-won history. Here are the 30 accounts I'd attack first. Here's the calibration I need from you on scoring weights before week 3."

Week 2 · Calibrate, plan the first cohort

  • Signal scoring calibrated against actual closed-won customers. Rubric adjusted based on Head of GTM's input.
  • First cohort of 30–40 accounts locked. Includes Paddy's original 34 candidates plus any high-signal additions from week 1's signal scrapes.
  • Outreach templates reviewed internally – send 5 sample drafts (insight-led, value-nugget, high-signal) to the Head of GTM and the CEO for tone calibration.
  • Pre-meeting research GPT and post-call transcript GPT (Function 10 from the experiments page) ported into the workflow so closing AEs start to see the operator role making their work easier from day one.

Week 3 · First outreach cohort live

  • 30–40 accounts in motion. Outreach sequenced across email + LinkedIn + phone, with SF-based ones flagged for potential in-person visits.
  • Function 6 (LinkedIn Engagement Harvester) goes live against Solidroad employee LinkedIn posts. First daily digest fires.
  • Function 5 (Job-Change Detector) connected to the named-account list and the existing customer / past-evaluator list. Daily Slack alerts begin.
  • First "the system found this" win – likely a CX leader job change triggering Variant C outreach within 24 hours. The first proof point.

Week 4 · Iterate, launch experiments 1–3

  • Reply data lands from the first cohort. Iterate Function 4 (Outreach Drafter) based on what's converting.
  • Experiment 1 (LinkedIn engagement → pipeline), Experiment 3 (CX leader 60-day window), and Experiment 10 (operator automation stack) formally launch with kill criteria documented.
  • Weekly review rhythm established. Friday afternoon with the Head of GTM. 30 min. What fired, what worked, what's broken, what's shipping next week.
End of month 1 success criterion. All six Functions live. 30+ accounts in motion. First Evaluating-stage account surfaced by the system. Weekly review rhythm holding. The closing AEs are using the pre-meeting brief and post-call transcript GPTs because they make their lives easier.
Days 31–60 · scale and prove

Month two. The system meets reality. The case gets made.

Weeks 5–6 · Second cohort, more experiments

  • Second batch of 50+ accounts goes into motion. Total active accounts crosses 80.
  • Experiment 2 (AI-agent-without-QA detector), Experiment 6 (SF office in-person motion), and Experiment 9 (conference speaker outreach) launch. First batch of each experiment tested against 15–20 prospects.
  • First Evaluating-stage account progresses to POC discussion. Operator runs the pre-meeting brief, closing team takes the demo.
  • Reply data from month one is rich enough to refine messaging by segment. Insight-led wins for some personas; value-nugget wins for others. The per-segment preferences get codified.

Weeks 7–8 · Scale what works, build the case

  • The two highest-leverage experiments from weeks 4–6 get formal scaling treatment. Resource allocation shifts toward whichever combination is producing.
  • The lower-leverage experiments get killed on their kill dates – the LinkedIn engagement, CX-leader, or AI-agent-without-QA play that's underperforming gets sunset, with the learnings written up.
  • Documentation push. Every Function spec'd with inputs, outputs, edge cases. Every experiment logged with hypothesis, method, result. Every account-to-account learning written down. The doc is part of the deliverable, not a quarter-end push.
  • Pull the numbers: accounts touched, replies, meetings booked, Evaluating-stage progressed, POCs in motion, reply rates by signal type. Write the one-pager the Head of GTM can share with the CEO.

Day 60 review

  • Formal review with the Head of GTM and the CEO.
  • Three questions on the table: (1) is the system producing? (2) which of the experiments has earned a permanent place in the playbook? (3) what's the right next hire – a second operator, an SDR-light role pairing with the system, or staying solo for another quarter?
  • If the answers to (1) and (2) are strong, the conversation moves to scaling: hire #2 for the function, new motion (international? BPO-specific?), additional Functions to ship in months 3–6.
  • If the answers are weaker, an honest conversation about why, and what would need to change.
End of month 2 success criterion. 80+ accounts in motion. 3+ Evaluating-stage accounts. 1–2 POCs in motion. At least two experiments killed on schedule with learnings logged. Documentation current enough to be useful to a second hire. The conversation moves from "is this role working" to "how do we scale it."
What this plan deliberately doesn't say

The honest caveats.

  • Pipeline numbers are projected, not promised. "3+ Evaluating-stage accounts in 60 days" is a hypothesis, not a quota. If the signals don't fire the way the take-home assumes, the numbers move. The discipline is being honest about that with the Head of GTM weekly, not fudging the data at the end of month two.
  • The plan is overweighted to the first 30 days. That's deliberate. The DRI model fails when month-one momentum doesn't materialise – the role gets second-guessed before it gets a chance to work. Ship the foundation fast and visibly so the conversation in month two is about results, not whether the bet was right.
  • Week one is aggressive. Shipping six Functions in five working days is genuinely ambitious. If any single Function turns out to need a week of its own (likely Function 6, the LinkedIn Engagement Harvester, which has the most fiddly scraping logic), the plan flexes. The commitment is "all six live by end of week 2" as a hard backstop.
  • There is no plan B in this document. If the operator-plus-system model isn't producing material pipeline by day 60, the right answer is to surface that honestly, not stretch the timeline to month four. The whole point of running this as experiments is that experiments are killable.
