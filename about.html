02 · doorblockade as proto-role

The same job, different scale.

When Paddy described the SDR-less factory on our call, I recognised it immediately, because it's the role I was already running. Here's the mapping.

The role Paddy describedWhat I did at DoorBlockade
Build the signal infrastructure Built three workflows running together. (1) A Lindy workflow that scraped Google Alerts for school security incidents, found the right contact (Director of Security / Superintendent), drafted a personalised email referencing the incident, waited two weeks, dropped it into my Gmail drafts for review. (2) A custom GPT that on command compiled a pre-meeting research brief on any prospect – web search, recent news, public statements, technographic clues – into a one-page format I could read on my phone before walking in. (3) A second custom GPT that ingested call transcripts after the meeting, scored them against criteria I cared about (objection patterns, decision-maker signals, technical fit), and drafted the follow-up email plus a next-action plan.
Run it on a tight target list Maintained a working list of ~200 schools and ~80 health systems, scored by recent incident, regional regulatory pressure, and procurement openness. Reviewed and adjusted weekly.
Pick up the phone when the system surfaces a moment Cold-called Chief Security Officers at health systems and Directors of Security at schools – sceptical, hard-to-reach buyers. Closed deals from those calls.
Be founder-mode in the role Was the founder. Wrote the deck. Took the demos. Ran the POCs. Handled implementation. Renewed the contracts.
The $6M outlier deal Before DoorBlockade, at the Notre Dame IDEA Center: spotted a signal in a press release, acted on it before the rest of the team noticed, walked it into a ~$6M placement deal in roughly a month. That's the modern outbound playbook in its purest form – signal → contact → conversation → close.
The honest gap. What I haven't done is run this seat inside a built sales organisation, with someone closing behind me at quota, on a defined ICP I didn't choose. That's the development goal, and it's the reason this role at Solidroad is the right next move for me – not because I need to learn outbound from scratch, but because I want to see what outstanding looks like when the surrounding machine is real.
03 · three moments

How I got here, in three.

Three moments that shaped how I think about the operator role. Each one is a story about the same instinct – read the signal, find the right person, build the system that scales the motion.

The $6M deal at the Notre Dame IDEA Center

Second year of undergrad, working at the Notre Dame IDEA Center on real corporate placement deals. The team was working through a target list the conventional way – phone, email, conferences. Methodical, but slow.

I spotted a signal in a public press release that the rest of the team hadn't acted on. Identified the right contact inside the organisation, wrote the outreach myself, got the meeting within a week. Closed a roughly $6M placement deal in about a month. The deal wasn't on the team's radar before the signal fired.

The motion the GTM-operator role is built around – read the signal, find the right person, write the message that gets the reply – is the motion I've been running since I was 21. The tooling has gotten better. The instinct is the same.

The cold email that started this conversation

April 16, 2026: Solidroad announces the $25M Series A. I wrote a personalised cold email to Mark the next morning – referenced the Series A, the role I'd seen on Ashby, and a specific reason I thought I'd be a fit. Sent it.

Mark replied. Ten-minute call with him. Thirty-minute call with Paddy. Paddy's follow-up email to Mark, with me cc'd: "I think we could find something groundbreaking, provided there is a good fit." Onsite scheduled.

If the question is whether I can run outbound for Solidroad, the answer is in the inbox the role is now hiring against.

Building DoorBlockade from zero

Hardware security startup. No brand, no playbook, no revenue. ICP was Directors of Security at schools and Chief Security Officers at health systems – sceptical buyers who didn't know they had a budget for the category.

I ran the full motion solo. Cold calls, cold emails, conferences, door-to-door visits in person. Built the Lindy workflow that scraped Google Alerts for security incidents and drafted personalised outreach automatically. Built a pre-meeting research GPT that compiled a one-page brief on any prospect on command. Built a second GPT that scored call transcripts after the fact and drafted the follow-up plan. The stack was scrappy, but it ran every day, and it produced.

Got to roughly $50K revenue in a few months, raised $450K, deployed hardware in real schools and health systems. The role we're discussing is the version of this with proper budget, infrastructure, and Clay behind it.

05 · what i'd want to hear

If this works for me at 18 months, what's true?

  • The SDR-less factory exists. There's a documented Clay Functions playbook anyone on the team can call. Pipeline-per-dollar materially beats the SDR benchmark.
  • Paddy and I have a working operating rhythm. Weekly review, monthly retro, quarterly experiments. He trusts me with progressively higher-stakes calls – major-account outreach, BPO conversations, maybe the early stages of his own deals.
  • The Solidroad GTM team is bigger because of work I helped land. At least 5–8 new logos that came through the system I built. At least one BPO deal where the system surfaced the moment, and I or Paddy converted it.
  • The role has earned a successor. There's a second seat to fill, and the doc I left makes the second hire faster than I was.
  • I haven't lost the phone half. The systems work hasn't pulled me out of the customer-facing motion. If anything the ratio is sharper – fewer, better-qualified conversations.
The honest version. The reason I want this seat isn't that I think outbound is easy and I'll crush it. It's that I've been doing the proto-version of it alone for two years, and I want to see what it looks like when the surrounding machine is real and the founders are people who've shipped before. That's why this role. That's why Solidroad.
